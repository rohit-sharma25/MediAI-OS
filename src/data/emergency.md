# Emergency Triage & First-Aid Guidelines

This document outlines standard triage protocols to categorize the severity of medical cases and provides first-aid guidance for acute situations in India.

---

## 1. The Emergency Triage Severity System

Triage is the process of determining the priority of patients' treatments based on the severity of their condition. We utilize a 4-Tier color-coded system:

### 🔴 RED: EMERGENCY (Immediate Life Threat)
* **Status**: Critical. The patient requires immediate medical intervention to survive.
* **Indicators**:
  * Complete cessation of breathing or heart pulse.
  * Sudden chest pain accompanied by left arm pain and sweating (suspected heart attack).
  * Major trauma or arterial bleeding that cannot be controlled.
  * Unconsciousness, persistent seizures, or severe breathing distress.
  * Severe anaphylaxis (allergic swelling/airway closure).
* **Action**: **Call 112 immediately. Begin CPR or stop-bleeding maneuvers if trained.**

### 🟠 ORANGE: URGENT (Potential Life/Limb Threat)
* **Status**: High Priority. The patient is stable but could deteriorate rapidly if not seen within 15–30 minutes.
* **Indicators**:
  * Moderate breathing difficulty or high wheezing.
  * Signs of stroke: Facial drooping, arm weakness, speech difficulty (FAST).
  * Severe abdominal pain, high fever in infants, or compound fractures.
  * Severe chemical burns or suspected poison ingestion.
* **Action**: **Seek immediate transport to the nearest emergency department.**

### 🟡 YELLOW: MODERATE (Non-Life Threatening)
* **Status**: Medium Priority. The patient requires medical attention but can wait up to 1–2 hours without immediate danger.
* **Indicators**:
  * Minor fractures, severe sprains, or deep cuts requiring stitches.
  * Moderate fever (102°F) that does not respond to paracetamol, but patient is fully alert.
  * Persistent vomiting or severe dehydration, moderate asthma flares.
* **Action**: **Visit an urgent care clinic or schedule a prompt doctor appointment.**

### 🟢 GREEN: MILD (Self-Limiting / Routine Care)
* **Status**: Low Priority. The condition is minor and can wait or be treated with self-care at home.
* **Indicators**:
  * Minor scrapes, bruises, mild sprains.
  * Low-grade fever, common cold, sore throat, or mild seasonal allergies.
  * Mild diarrhea, insect bites, or routine dressing changes.
* **Action**: **Use standard home care protocols, rest, and consult a clinic if symptoms persist.**

## 2. Basic First-Aid Protocols

### Cardiopulmonary Resuscitation (CPR)
* **Step 1**: Check responsiveness. Tap shoulder and shout, "Are you okay?"
* **Step 2**: Check for breathing. If not breathing, call 112/108.
* **Step 3**: Position hands. Place one hand on the center of the chest, other hand on top, locking fingers.
* **Step 4**: Give compressions. Push hard and fast (100–120 compressions per minute) at least 2 inches deep. Let the chest rise completely between compressions.

### Control of Severe Bleeding
* **Step 1**: Apply direct, firm pressure on the wound using a clean cloth or sterile dressing.
* **Step 2**: Keep pressure applied consistently. Do not lift the cloth to check.
* **Step 3**: If blood seeps through, place another cloth on top and keep pressing.
* **Step 4**: Elevate the limb above the level of the heart if possible.

## 3. Important Emergency Hotlines in India
* **112**: **National Emergency Number (All-in-one helpline for Police, Fire, and Ambulance)**
* **108**: **Disaster and Medical Emergency Response (Free State-sponsored ambulance systems)**
* **102**: **National Ambulance Service (Specifically targeted for pregnant women and neonates)**
* **104**: **State Health Helpline / Medical Consultation**
