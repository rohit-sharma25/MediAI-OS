# General Medical & Preventive Health Reference

This reference covers common infectious diseases, public health guidelines, and general wellness practices suited to the Indian climate and lifestyle.

---

## 1. Common Indian Infectious Diseases

### Fever (General Overview)
* **Description**: A temporary increase in body temperature, usually in response to an infection. Normal temperature is around 98.6°F (37°C); a fever is defined as 100.4°F (38°C) or higher.
* **First Aid**: Rest, cool compress on the forehead, and adequate hydration. Avoid self-prescribing strong antibiotics.

### Dengue Fever (Breakbone Fever)
* **Vector**: Transmitted by the *Aedes aegypti* mosquito, which typically bites during daytime.
* **Key Symptoms**: High fever, severe headache, retro-orbital pain (pain behind the eyes), joint and muscle pain, rash, and minor bleeding (nose/gums).
* **Warning Signs**: Severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, mucosal bleeding, rapid drop in platelet count (Thrombocytopenia), and extreme fatigue.
* **Care**: Supportive care including aggressive hydration (water, ORS, coconut water). Monitor platelet counts closely.

### Malaria
* **Vector**: Transmitted by the female *Anopheles* mosquito.
* **Key Symptoms**: Cyclical high fever with intense chills (rigors) followed by sweating, headache, muscle pain, fatigue, and sometimes mild jaundice.
* **Types**: *Plasmodium vivax* (most common in India) and *Plasmodium falciparum* (severe form; can cause cerebral malaria).
* **Care**: Requires prompt blood test (smear/rapid antigen test) and professional anti-malarial treatment.

### Typhoid (Enteric Fever)
* **Pathogen**: *Salmonella typhi* bacteria, transmitted through contaminated food and water.
* **Key Symptoms**: Step-ladder pattern fever (increases daily), severe headache, abdominal pain, diarrhea or constipation, dry cough, and rose-colored spots on the chest.
* **Care**: Confirmed via blood culture (Widal test is less reliable). Requires specific course of antibiotics prescribed by a physician.

## 2. Preventive Wellness & Immunity

### Hydration in the Indian Climate
* **Optimal Intake**: 2.5 to 3.5 liters of clean water daily. This intake should be increased during hot summer months or when exercising.
* **Electrolytes**: In cases of heat exhaustion, replenish with Oral Rehydration Salts (ORS) or fresh coconut water.
* **Benefits**: Supports cellular function, prevents kidney stones, aids digestion, and keeps skin healthy.

### Nutrition and Balanced Diet
* **Micronutrients**: Ensure adequate zinc, vitamin C, and vitamin D. Indian diets can be supplemented with green leafy vegetables, citrus fruits (amla, oranges), yogurt, and lentils.
* **Spices with Medicinal Benefits**:
  * **Turmeric (Haldi)**: Contains curcumin, a potent anti-inflammatory compound.
  * **Ginger & Garlic**: Provide natural antimicrobial and cardiovascular support.
* **Fiber**: Essential for maintaining a healthy gut microbiome, which is closely linked to immune system function.

### Restorative Sleep
* **Duration**: 7 to 9 hours of uninterrupted sleep every night.
* **Mechanism**: During deep sleep, the immune system releases cytokines (proteins that target infections and inflammation). Chronic sleep deprivation reduces immune efficiency.

### Immunity Strengthening
* **Exercise**: Moderate-intensity daily exercise increases white blood cell circulation.
* **Stress Management**: High stress increases cortisol, suppressing immune responses and making the body susceptible to infections.
